# Gradopus Labs Project Submission & Assessment Policy

- **Version:** 1.1
- **Effective date:** To be set after legal review
- **Last updated:** 23 October 2026
- **Operated by:** Gradopus Labs, a sole proprietorship based in Maharashtra, India
- **Contact:** <support.gradopuslabs@gmail.com>

This policy explains how tasks are set, how you submit your work, how it is assessed, and how assessment decides
whether you complete a program. It applies to every Gradopus Labs program and is read together with the
[Internship Policy](./internship-policy.md) and the [Student Terms & Conditions](./terms-and-conditions.md).

## 1. Purpose and scope

The purpose of this policy is to make assessment **transparent, consistent and fair**. It applies to:

- every student taking part in a Gradopus Labs program;
- every task, submission and review in those programs;
- the reviewers and mentors who assess work on behalf of Gradopus Labs.

Each task brief may add task-specific requirements. A task brief can make requirements more specific, but it
cannot remove a protection this policy gives you.

## 2. Definitions

- **Assignment** (or **task**): a piece of work set in a program, with a brief, submission requirements and
  acceptance criteria.
- **Task brief:** the written instructions for an assignment, shown with the task.
- **Deliverable:** what the task brief asks you to produce, such as working code, a deployed application or
  documentation.
- **Submission:** what you send for review in response to a task, such as a text answer or a project link. Each
  time you submit, a new **attempt** is recorded.
- **Reviewer:** a person who assesses submissions on behalf of Gradopus Labs. A reviewer may also be your mentor.
- **Revision:** a new attempt that you submit after a reviewer requests changes or rejects a submission.
- **Required task:** a task that must be completed to complete the program. **Optional tasks** never affect
  completion.
- **Completion:** meeting all of the completion requirements published for a program (section 22).

## 3. How tasks are assigned and communicated

- Tasks are organised into **modules** within each program and are shown in your student dashboard once you are
  enrolled.
- Each task shows its brief, what to submit, its acceptance criteria, whether it is required or optional, and its
  deadline, if it has one.
- If a task is changed after you have started it, we will tell you, and the change will not be applied to a
  submission you have already made without your agreement.
- Ask your mentor or reviewer if anything in a brief is unclear. Asking for clarification is never held against you.
  `[TO BE CONFIRMED: the channel for task questions]`

## 4. Task briefs and acceptance criteria

Every task brief will state, **before you start**:

- what you are expected to build or do, and what you should learn from it;
- the **submission method** (section 7) and which evidence is required;
- the **acceptance criteria** your work will be assessed against;
- whether the task is **required** or **optional**;
- any rules on **AI tools** (section 13) and **third-party code** (section 14);
- whether the task's work may be published publicly (section 24);
- its deadline, if any (section 6).

You will be assessed **only** against the criteria published for the task and the completion requirements of your
program. A reviewer will not reject your work for failing a requirement that was not stated.

## 5. Mandatory and task-specific requirements

**These apply to every submission:**

- It must be your own work, with honest attribution (sections 12 to 14).
- It must not contain passwords, API keys, tokens, other secrets or other people's personal data (section 15).
- Any link must open securely (`https://`) and be accessible to the reviewer until the review is complete.

**These apply only when a task brief asks for them:**

- a GitHub repository (section 8);
- a live deployment or demonstration (section 9);
- a README or setup instructions (section 10);
- screenshots, recordings, progress reports or written explanations (section 11).

## 6. Deadlines, milestones and extensions

- A task may have a deadline, shown with the task. Some programs may also set milestones, which will be stated in
  the internship details. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: whether programs use milestones]`
- Deadlines are set so that the work can reasonably be completed within the time commitment published for the
  program.
- **Late submissions are still reviewed**, and the reviewer can see when the work was submitted.
  `[TO BE CONFIRMED: whether lateness affects the assessment of any task, and any grace period]`
- **Extensions:** if you need more time for a genuine reason, such as illness, a family emergency, examinations or
  a disability-related need, ask before the deadline where you can. Reasonable requests will be considered fairly
  and answered in writing.
- Missing a deadline is never, on its own, treated as misconduct.

## 7. Supported submission methods

The website currently accepts two kinds of submission:

- **Text answers**, up to 10,000 characters, for example a written explanation or a progress report;
- **Project links** starting with `https://`, for example a GitHub repository or a live deployment URL.

File uploads are **not available** on the website. If a task needs screenshots, a recording or a document, the task
brief will say how to provide it, for example by including it in your repository or sharing a link to it.

The website never opens or downloads the links you submit. Reviewers open them in their own browsers.

## 8. GitHub repository requirements

When a task asks for a repository:

- Submit the repository URL **only when the task asks for it**.
- A **private repository is acceptable**. Give the reviewer access as the task brief explains, for example by
  inviting the reviewer's account as a collaborator with read access. You do not have to make work public to have it
  reviewed. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: the reviewer account to invite]`
- Grant only the access needed for the review, and remove it after the review if you wish.
- Never share your GitHub password, personal access tokens or other credentials. Gradopus Labs will never ask for
  them.
- Your commit history helps show your own contribution. Do not rewrite or delete history after submitting until the
  review is complete, unless you need to remove a secret and you tell your reviewer.
- The repository must not contain secrets or confidential data (section 15).
- Do not upload code that belongs to an employer, client, college or anyone else unless you are authorised to.
- Follow the licences of any code you include (section 14).

## 9. Live deployments and demonstrations

When a task asks for a live deployment or a demonstration:

- Submit the deployment URL **only when the task asks for it**, and keep it working until the review is complete.
- A live deployment is **evidence that the application works**. It is not, by itself, proof of authorship or
  originality.
- You may be asked to **explain your code or demonstrate features** in a short, reasonable review, for example a
  call or a recorded walkthrough. This helps the reviewer assess your understanding and your own contribution.
  `[TO BE CONFIRMED: how demonstrations are held, e.g. live call or recording]`
- Use only free or approved hosting. Do not use paid services in the name of Gradopus Labs without written
  permission.
- Do not put real personal data into a deployed task project unless the task requires it.

## 10. README documentation and setup instructions

When a task asks for documentation, a README should usually explain:

- what the project does;
- how to install, configure and run it, including any environment variables, but **never their real secret
  values**;
- how to run any tests;
- the main technical decisions and any known limitations;
- the third-party code, templates and AI assistance you used, where the task asks for it.

## 11. Screenshots, recordings and supporting evidence

- Provide screenshots, recordings, progress reports or explanations **only when the task asks for them**.
- Make sure they do not show passwords, tokens, private messages, other people's personal information or anything
  confidential.
- Evidence must be genuine. Edited or staged evidence that misrepresents what your work does is falsified evidence
  (section 20).

## 12. Originality and attribution

- Your submission must show **your own** work and understanding.
- Credit any code, tutorials, articles, templates, datasets or other material you used, in the way the task asks
  and as the material's licence requires.
- In group tasks, each member must be able to show their own contribution.
- Using properly credited material is not plagiarism. Presenting someone else's work as your own is.

## 13. AI coding assistants and generated code

Using AI tools is **not automatically misconduct**. The rules are:

1. **Follow the task brief.** Each task states whether AI tools may be used and how. Where a brief allows AI
   assistance, you may use it within those limits. Where a brief does not allow it, you must not.
   `[TO BE CONFIRMED: the default rule when a task brief says nothing about AI tools]`
2. **Disclose your use** in the way the task asks, for example a short note in your README saying which tool you
   used and for what.
3. **Understand what you submit.** You must be able to explain, test and change any code you submit, including
   generated code.
4. **Represent your contribution honestly.** Submitting AI-generated work as your own where the task does not allow
   it, or without disclosing it, is misrepresentation.

You will **never** be asked to share your AI-tool account, its password or your private chat history. If a reviewer
needs to understand how you used a tool, they may ask you to explain it.

The website does not use automated AI-detection or plagiarism-detection software. If Gradopus Labs uses any such
tool, it will be checked for reliability before use, and its result will never on its own be treated as proof of
misconduct. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: whether reviewers will use any detection tools]`

## 14. Open-source libraries, templates and third-party components

- You may use libraries, frameworks, templates and other third-party components unless the task brief says
  otherwise.
- Use them only as their licence allows, and keep the licence and copyright notices the licence requires.
- Credit them as the task asks, for example in your README.
- Do not use code you do not have the right to use, such as leaked, pirated or confidential code.
- Using a template or starter project is allowed only where the task permits it. Your submission must make clear
  which parts you wrote.

## 15. Confidentiality, credentials and personal data

- Never include passwords, API keys, private tokens, private certificates or other secrets in a submission,
  repository, deployment, screenshot or recording. Use environment variables or a local file that is not committed.
- If you commit a secret by mistake, tell your reviewer immediately and change the secret. Removing it from the
  latest commit is not enough, because it remains in the history.
- Do not include other people's personal data unless the task requires it, and then only what is needed.
- Confidential material from Gradopus Labs or a client must not be published (see the Intellectual Property & Code of
  Conduct).

## 16. Assessment criteria and scoring transparency

- Each task is assessed against its own published **acceptance criteria**. There is no universal passing score.
- A reviewer records one of three outcomes:
  - **Accepted:** the submission meets the task's criteria;
  - **Changes requested:** it needs specific improvements, which the feedback explains;
  - **Rejected:** it does not meet the criteria, for example because it is incomplete, inaccessible or not your own
    work.
- A reviewer may also record a **score out of 100** for a task. Scores give you feedback; unless the task brief says
  so, a score does not decide whether a task is accepted. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: whether scores are used, and whether
  any task uses a minimum score]`
- The same criteria apply to every student on the same task.

## 17. Aspects of work that may be assessed

A task's criteria may cover some or all of the following, as stated in the brief:

- **Correctness and functionality:** the work does what the task asks.
- **Code quality:** readable, organised and maintainable code.
- **Documentation:** clear setup and usage instructions.
- **Accessibility:** for user interfaces, whether people with disabilities can use them, where the task asks.
- **Security:** no exposed secrets and sensible handling of input and data, where relevant.
- **Originality and attribution:** honest representation of your own work.
- **Understanding:** your ability to explain your implementation and decisions.

A task is never assessed on an aspect it does not mention.

## 18. Feedback and revisions

- Every "Changes requested" or "Rejected" outcome comes with **written feedback** explaining why and, where possible,
  what to improve.
- After "Changes requested" or "Rejected", you may submit a **revision**. Earlier attempts and their feedback are
  kept and shown to you and the reviewer.
- The website allows up to **10 attempts** per task. A task brief may set a lower limit, which will be shown with
  the task. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: whether any task sets a lower limit]`
- While a submission is waiting for review, you cannot submit another attempt for that task. Once a submission is
  accepted, the task is complete and no further attempts are needed.
- Reviewers aim to review submissions promptly. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: expected review time]`
- A reviewer may correct the written feedback or score after a decision; you will be able to see the updated
  feedback.

## 19. Failed, incomplete, late or inaccessible submissions

- **Incomplete** work is usually given "Changes requested" with feedback, so you can finish it.
- **Late** work is still reviewed (section 6).
- **Inaccessible** work (for example a broken link or a private repository the reviewer cannot open) cannot be
  accepted. The reviewer will request changes and explain what is needed, so you can fix access and resubmit.
- **Rejected** work can be revised and resubmitted, within the attempt limit.
- If you reach the attempt limit for a required task without an accepted submission, contact your reviewer. The
  case will be reviewed individually. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: what happens when the attempt limit is reached]`

## 20. Plagiarism, copied work, falsified evidence and misconduct

The following are academic misconduct:

- copying another person's work, including another student's, and presenting it as your own;
- submitting work written for you by someone else, paid or unpaid;
- sharing your solutions so that others can submit them as their own, unless the task allows it;
- falsifying evidence, such as commit history, screenshots, recordings, deployment URLs or demonstrations;
- submitting AI-generated work where the task does not allow it, or without the disclosure the task requires.

**How concerns are handled:**

1. The reviewer tells you, in writing, what the concern is and the evidence for it.
2. You have a fair opportunity to respond, for example by explaining your work or showing your process.
3. A decision is made and explained to you in writing.

Honest mistakes, such as a missing credit, are treated differently from deliberate or repeated misconduct. They will
usually lead to guidance and a request to correct the submission.

Possible outcomes include a request to correct or redo the task, a rejected submission or, for serious or repeated
misconduct, the steps in the Student Terms & Conditions. There are **no fines**. A certificate obtained through
misconduct may be revoked, but only through the process in the Certificate Issuance & Verification Policy.

## 21. Appeals and reassessment requests

If you think a review outcome is wrong or unfair:

1. **Ask your reviewer** first to explain the decision, if you are comfortable doing so.
2. **Request a reassessment** in writing to <support.gradopuslabs@gmail.com> within `[TO BE CONFIRMED: number of
   days]` of the decision. Say which task it is, which criteria you think were applied wrongly, and why.
3. Where possible, the reassessment is carried out by **someone who did not make the original decision**.
4. You will receive a written outcome with reasons. If the reassessment finds that your submission met the criteria,
   the outcome will be corrected in our records. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: response time]`

Requesting a reassessment will never count against you. If you are still not satisfied, you can raise a grievance
(section 25).

## 22. Completion decisions and certificate eligibility

You complete a program when you meet **all** of its published completion requirements:

1. every **required** task has an **accepted** review or, for a non-assessed activity such as a reading module, is
   marked as done in the way the task describes; and
2. any other requirement stated in the program's internship details.
   `[TO BE CONFIRMED: any additional completion requirements per program]`

- Project submissions count towards completion **only once accepted**. Submitting a link does not by itself
  complete a task.
- Optional tasks never affect completion.
- Paying the enrollment fee pays for taking part in the program. It does **not** mean your work will be accepted or
  that you will receive a certificate.
- Not every student completes every program, and a certificate is issued only when the completion requirements are
  met. There is **no fee** for a certificate.

Certificate issuance is explained in the Certificate Issuance & Verification Policy.

## 23. Retention, privacy and use of project evidence

- Your submissions, attempts, feedback and review outcomes are stored so that you and the reviewers can see the
  history of your work, and so that completion and certificates can be checked later.
- Only you and the Gradopus Labs team can see your submissions. They are not made public by Gradopus Labs.
- How long submissions are kept, and how you can ask for them to be deleted, is explained in the
  [Privacy Policy](./privacy-policy.md). Submissions are currently kept with no automatic deletion.
  `[TO BE CONFIRMED: retention period for submissions]`

## 24. Intellectual property and publication permissions

- Submitting work for review does **not** transfer ownership of it to Gradopus Labs. It gives Gradopus Labs
  permission to store, access, review and assess the work and keep a record of it, only for running the program,
  verifying completion and certificates, handling disputes and keeping records the law requires.
- Who owns work created in a program is explained in the Intellectual Property & Code of Conduct.
- Each task brief states whether you may publish the work publicly, for example in a public repository or portfolio.
  Work that includes confidential or client material must not be published without written permission.
- Gradopus Labs will not use your work, name, photograph or testimonial in promotional material without your
  separate, written permission. Refusing will not affect your assessment or certificate.

## 25. Grievances and contact

If your concern is not resolved through a reassessment, you can raise a formal grievance as described in the
[Student Terms & Conditions](./terms-and-conditions.md), by writing to <support.gradopuslabs@gmail.com>. You will not be penalised for raising a genuine concern.
