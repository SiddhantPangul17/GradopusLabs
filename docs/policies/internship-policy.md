This policy explains how Gradopus Labs internships work, from joining a program to completing it.

## 1. About Gradopus Labs and this policy

Gradopus Labs is an India-based brand offering practical, project-based software development internships and
technology learning.

Gradopus Labs is **not** a university, college, government body or accredited institution, and it is not
incorporated as a company. Taking part in a Gradopus Labs internship does not give you a degree, academic credit,
a government-recognised qualification or an accredited credential.

In this policy, "we", "us" and "our" mean Gradopus Labs and the person who operates it. "You" means a student who
applies for, or takes part in, a Gradopus Labs internship.

## 2. Purpose and scope

This policy explains how our internships work: how you apply, what you will be asked to do, how your work is
reviewed, when a certificate may be issued and what you can do if something goes wrong.

It applies to every applicant and intern in a Gradopus Labs internship, and to the mentors and reviewers who
work with them.

Each internship also has its own **internship details**, shown to you in writing before you enroll or accept a
place, for example on the program's page. They set out the specific terms of that internship, such as its fee,
duration, weekly time commitment, tasks and completion requirements. If the internship details and this policy
disagree, the internship details apply to that internship, except that they cannot remove any protection this
policy or the law gives you.

Nothing in this policy limits any right you have under the law.

## 3. Nature of the internship program

Our internships are structured learning experiences. You work on software development tasks, receive guidance
from a mentor and have your work reviewed against criteria that are shared with you in advance.

The internship details for each internship will state clearly:

- what you will learn and the kind of work you will do;
- the **enrollment fee** for the program (section 3.1);
- whether your work is limited to practice tasks or also includes contributing to a real product or client
  project.

## 4. Eligibility and application process

**Who can join.** You must be **18 or older**. Each internship also lists any other eligibility requirements, such
as the skills or prior knowledge needed to follow the tasks. Requirements are limited to what is genuinely needed
for the internship. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: other eligibility requirements per internship]`

**Equal opportunity.** We do not exclude or treat any applicant less favourably because of gender, gender
identity, sexual orientation, caste, religion, region, place of birth, language, disability, marital status,
pregnancy, family background or economic background. Where a skill is required, we assess the skill itself, not
the name of your college or how you acquired it.

**How to join.** All programs are currently **open enrollment**: you create an account, review the internship details
on the program's page, and enroll by paying the enrollment fee. There is no application or selection step. If a
program ever requires an application, its page will say so, and this policy and the Privacy Policy will be updated
first.

**What we ask for.** To create an account, we ask only for your name, email address and a password. We do not ask
for identity documents, or for information about your religion, caste, health or finances. The
[Privacy Policy](./privacy-policy.md) explains how this information is handled.

## 5. Selection and onboarding

**Selection.** There is currently no selection step, because all programs are open enrollment (section 4). If an
application-based program is introduced, its selection criteria will be published before applications open.

**Before you commit.** Whether you enroll directly or are offered a place after applying, you will see the
internship details in writing before you pay or accept. Read them carefully. You are not committed to anything
until you pay or accept.

**Onboarding.** Before your first task, you will receive:

- your start date and the name or role of your mentor;
- the communication channels to use;
- the tasks or modules you are expected to complete, with their review criteria;
- the completion requirements for the internship;
- a link to this policy and the related policies listed in section 19.

## 9. Project submission and review

**What you may be asked to submit.** Depending on the task:

- a link to a **GitHub repository** (or another code host named in the task);
- a **live deployment URL**, where the task requires one;
- **project documentation**, such as a README explaining how to set up and use your project;
- a **demonstration**, for example a short recorded walkthrough or a live demo with your mentor;
- other evidence needed to show **your own contribution**, such as your commit history.

**Submission rules.**

- Links must open securely (`https://`) and be accessible to the reviewer. If a repository is private, give the
  reviewer access as the task instructs; you do not have to make work public to have it reviewed.
- Work you submit must be your own. Credit any third-party code, libraries, tutorials or other resources you use,
  as the task instructions require. Follow the task's instructions on the use of AI coding assistants and disclose
  where you have used them. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: the general rule on AI-assisted work]`
- In group tasks, each member must be able to show their own contribution.
- Never include passwords, API keys, access tokens or other people's personal information in a repository,
  deployment or document you submit.
- Keep your submitted links working until the review is complete. If a link stops working, the reviewer may be
  unable to accept the submission.

**Review.** A reviewer assesses each project submission against the criteria published for that task. Submitting a
link, a file or a demonstration does **not** by itself complete a task. Project submissions count towards
completion only once a reviewer **accepts** them.

A reviewed submission will be marked as one of:

- **Accepted**: it meets the task's criteria;
- **Changes requested**: it needs specific improvements, which the feedback will explain;
- **Rejected**: it does not meet the criteria, for example because it is incomplete or not your own work.

After changes are requested or a submission is rejected, you may submit a new attempt. A task may limit the
number of attempts; if so, the limit is shown on the task. Earlier attempts and their feedback are kept.

1. every **required** task has an **accepted** review (or, for a non-assessed activity such as a reading module, is
   marked as done in the way the task describes); and
2. any other requirement stated in your internship details.

Optional tasks do not affect completion.

Enrolling, paying the enrollment fee, attending sessions or submitting work does not, by itself, complete an

## 13. Certificate eligibility and limitations

**When a certificate may be issued.** A Certificate of Completion may be issued once you have met all of the
completion requirements in section 12 and our records confirm this. Certificates are issued by Gradopus Labs; you
cannot generate your own.

- There is **no fee** for a certificate.
- If your enrollment fee was refunded (which happens only in the limited cases in the Payment, Refund & Stipend
  Policy, such as a program cancelled by Gradopus Labs), a certificate is not issued for that enrollment.
- An issued certificate is never revoked because of a payment or administrative dispute.

**What a certificate shows.** A certificate records only what is documented in our records: your name, the program
you completed, the relevant dates and a unique certificate ID. Anyone can check a certificate's status on our
verification page, which shows only limited details.

**What a certificate does not mean.**

- It is **not** a degree, a university or government-recognised qualification or an accredited credential.
- It does **not** guarantee a job, a job interview, admission to further study or any other outcome, and it does
  not mean that any employer or institution endorses you.
- It does **not** describe skills, roles or achievements that are not documented in our records.

**Academic credit.** If you hope to use the internship for credit at your college, check with your college before
you apply. Gradopus Labs cannot promise that any college will accept it.

**Letters of recommendation** are not part of the internship and are given only at a mentor's discretion.

**Corrections and revocation.** A certificate with an error (such as a misspelled name) can be corrected and
reissued. A certificate obtained through plagiarised, falsified or fraudulent work may be revoked, after a fair
process. Details are in the Certificate Issuance & Verification Policy.

## 15. Confidentiality, security and responsible use of code

**Confidential information.** During an internship you may be given information that is not public, such as
private repositories, unreleased product details, or information belonging to Gradopus Labs or a client. You must:

- use it only for your internship tasks;
- not share, publish or copy it outside the internship;
- return or delete it, and give up any access to it, when your internship ends or when asked.

**Publishing and showcasing your work.** You may not publish, upload to a public repository or include in a
portfolio any code, documentation or other material that is confidential or belongs to a client unless you have
received **written permission** from Gradopus Labs. Who owns work created during an internship, and when you may
show it publicly, is set out in the Intellectual Property & Code of Conduct and, where relevant, your internship
details or a separate written agreement, before you start any task they apply to. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: ownership and showcasing rules for practice
tasks and for client or product work]`

You may always describe, in general terms, the skills you used and the kind of work you did, for example on your
CV, as long as you do not reveal confidential information.

**Security.**

- Never commit passwords, API keys, tokens or other secrets to a repository. If you do so by mistake, tell your
  mentor immediately so the secret can be changed.
- Use any access you are given (for example to a repository or server) only for your assigned tasks, and do not
  share it.
- Do not test, scan or attempt to access any system, account or data that you have not been explicitly authorised
  to use.
- Do not write or submit malicious code.

**Third-party code and licences.** Respect the licence terms of open-source and other third-party code you use, and
credit it as the licence and the task require.

## 16. Accessibility and reasonable accommodations

We want every intern to be able to take part fully. If you have a disability, a health condition or another
circumstance that affects how you can take part, you can ask for a reasonable accommodation at any time, including
before you apply.

**How to ask.** Contact us using the details in section 18 and describe what would help. Examples include extra time
for a task, a different format for materials or sessions, captions or transcripts, flexible session times, or a
different way to demonstrate your work.

**How requests are handled.**

- You only need to share the information needed to understand what support would help. You do not have to
  disclose a diagnosis.
- Requests are kept confidential and shared only with the people who need to know in order to arrange the
  accommodation.
- We will discuss the request with you and respond in writing.
  `[TO BE CONFIRMED: expected time to respond]`
- If we cannot provide exactly what you asked for, we will explain why and try to agree an alternative.
- Asking for an accommodation will never affect your selection, evaluation or certificate.

## 17. Withdrawal, termination and pending submissions

**If you want to withdraw.** You may withdraw from an internship at any time by telling us in writing. You do not
have to give a reason, although feedback is welcome.

**If we need to end an internship.** We may end your participation for serious or repeated breaches of this policy,
such as plagiarism, harassment or a security breach. Before making a decision, we will:

1. tell you in writing what the concern is;
2. give you a fair opportunity to respond;
3. tell you the decision and the reasons in writing.

Where there is an immediate risk to people, systems or confidential information, your access may be suspended
while the concern is looked into. A suspension is not a finding that you did anything wrong.

We may also end an internship early for reasons that are not your fault, for example if a program is
discontinued. If that happens, we will give you as much notice as possible, and your enrollment fee will be refunded
as set out in the Payment, Refund & Stipend Policy.

**What happens to your work and records.**

- Reviews already completed stay on your record.
- Submissions still waiting for review when an internship ends will be: `[TO BE CONFIRMED: reviewed, or closed
  without review]`. You will be told which.
- A certificate is issued only if you met all of the completion requirements before the internship ended.
- Enrollment fees are not refunded when you withdraw or your participation is ended for misconduct, except in the
  cases set out in the Payment, Refund & Stipend Policy.
- You must return or delete confidential material, as described in section 15.

**Appeals.** You can ask for a termination decision to be reconsidered using the process in section 18.

## 18. Grievances and contact information

termination decision or the behaviour of anyone involved, please raise it with us.

1. **Start informally**, where you feel comfortable doing so, by raising it with your mentor.
2. **Raise a formal grievance** by writing to <support.gradopuslabs@gmail.com>. Include your name, the
   internship, what happened and what outcome you are seeking.
3. We will acknowledge your grievance and respond in writing with a decision and the reasons for it.
   `[TO BE CONFIRMED: acknowledgement and response timelines]`
4. If you are not satisfied, you can ask for the decision to be reviewed by someone not involved in the original
   decision, where possible. `[TO BE CONFIRMED: who handles escalated grievances]`

You can skip the informal step for any reason, and you should go straight to a formal grievance for any complaint
of harassment.

You will not be penalised for raising a concern in good faith. This process does not affect any right you have to
seek other remedies available under law.
